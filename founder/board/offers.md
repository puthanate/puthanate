# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The dream outcome is real.** The heir keeps the family land, earns from it without learning to farm, and can trust the numbers. The Offers lens rates that outcome highly.
- **The heir has almost nothing to do.** A plain-Thai weekly LINE report, sealed weighing and photos. Low effort helps the value equation.
- **The offer is premium, not cheap,** and it promises accountability for what the company controls. That is the start of a guarantee.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The payer is not the person in pain, and the payoff is years away.** In Phase A the parents pay ฿40,000, installation and ฿3,000 a month, yet they already farm well and are on the land. The heir has the real pain but sees the payoff only at a handover that "could be many years" away. That means a long time to result for one buyer and a doubtful outcome for the other.
2. **There is no proof, so buyers will doubt it works.** There are no grower partners signed, no farming know-how on the team and no yield data. A buyer in Phase B hands an inheritance to strangers, so whether they believe the promise decides the sale.
3. **Nobody knows how big the market is.** We do not know how many families are in this situation, whether they have money to spare, or how to reach them. A weak market is the most common reason this lens passes. *(/founder-consumer, /founder-competitors)*

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Are there absent heirs **today** whose inherited land is being run badly? Could you sell Phase B to them first?
- Who pays, the parents or the children? Would a child in Bangkok pay for Phase A as insurance for the handover? *(/founder-consumer)*
- What does installation cost per plot, and how long does a family take to earn it back? *(/founder-cfo)*
- What proof can you show from one farm in one season?
- What exactly will you guarantee? For example, a refund of the month's fee if a report is late or a weighing is not sealed.
- Who already manages durian or rubber land for owners who live away, and on what terms? *(/founder-competitors)*

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Find real families where the heir is already away or the handover is close, and who say they would pay. Lead with the pain they have now.
2. Decide who pays, and price each phase against what that person gets, on their own timeline.
3. Sign a trusted grower partner and an agronomist before you sell Phase B.
4. Run the pilot farm for one season, with sealed harvest records and sample weekly reports.
5. Write down a guarantee on what the company controls, with its cost checked by /founder-cfo.
