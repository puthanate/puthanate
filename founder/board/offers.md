# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The pain is specific and has a money number behind it.** Theft at the buying yard, short-weighing, grade switching, encroachment and sibling deadlock are concrete problems, not "better farm management". If leakage is real, the offer pays for itself, and that is the strongest pitch this lens knows.
- **The dream outcome is emotional as well as financial:** keep the family land, stay in control, stop being cheated, without having to farm. The founder's choice not to take over management fits what the buyer says they want.
- **It is priced as a premium, not a commodity.** Nothing here competes on being cheaper.
- **Parts of the stack are cheap to deliver:** free satellite imagery, the weekly LINE update, family voting and playbooks cost little per plot once built.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No proof of likelihood.** The buyer is asked to believe a team with no farming know-how can find losses they cannot see from Bangkok. With no case study, no measured leakage and no guarantee, perceived likelihood is near zero, and that is where this lens says most offers die.
2. **The price may exceed the value on smaller plots.** ฿40,000 upfront plus ฿36,000 a year plus an IoT install of unknown cost, measured against plot income nobody has stated. If a plot earns less than the fee recovers, the offer is a cost, not a profit. Plot income and leakage rates are open questions for **/founder-cfo**; willingness to pay is for **/founder-consumer**.
3. **The market is hard to reach and its size is unknown.** Heirs are scattered, and lawyers, the Land Office and banks are unproven channels. Number of target heirs and cost to acquire one are open questions for **/founder-competitors** and **/founder-consumer**. A weak or unreachable market sinks a good offer.

Also noted: time to value is slow (a yield model needs a season to show a gap), and the caretaker may resist the scale, which raises effort for the owner.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- On what plot size and crop does the fee cost less than the loss it stops?
- What proof can you show a buyer before they pay ฿40,000?
- What does the owner get in the first 30 days?
- What is the guarantee, and can you afford it?
- Who is the one segment (crop, plot size, situation) with the most pain and money?
- If you dropped everything except the harvest scale and the weekly report, would anyone still buy?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Measure real leakage on 3 to 5 plots (pilot, free or at cost) and turn the result into a case study.
2. Lead with one narrow offer for the highest-value crop (likely durian), with a minimum plot size where the numbers work, confirmed by /founder-cfo.
3. Add a guarantee tied to the outcome, such as the Health Check fee credited back if it finds no recoverable loss.
4. Deliver a visible result in the first 30 days (boundary survey, tree count, Land Statement) so time to value is short.
5. Confirm with /founder-consumer that heirs will pay the Health Check price before building the IoT layer.
