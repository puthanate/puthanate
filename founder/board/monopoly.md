# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small, ownable starting market.** Absentee heirs of durian and rubber land in Surat Thani who will never sell. It is narrow enough to dominate, with clear next steps to more provinces and crops.
- **A partial secret.** These owners are buying trust, not yield. Sealed weighing and honest LINE reports in bad years answer the fear of being cheated, which agronomy alone does not.
- **A believable "why now".** Land passing to Bangkok-based heirs, EU Deforestation Regulation traceability, and cheap AI and sensors all arrive at once.
- **A possible data advantage** if plot records compound over time.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No advantage over the people doing the work.** Grower partners farm, and a commercial AI model runs on free data. Nothing is ten times better than an honest local manager with a phone. What stops a partner from dealing with the heir directly in month six?
2. **Distribution is unknown.** The first customer is family. How customers 2 to 100 arrive from Bangkok, and what each costs, for a ฿40,000 check with no promised result, has no answer yet.
3. **The network effect is too small to count.** Ten to twenty plots will not make the agents meaningfully better. Until the plot count is far larger, the ecosystem is a cost, not a moat.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who already manages or buys from absentee-owned orchards in Surat Thani: brokers, collectors, cooperatives, relatives? (/founder-competitors)
- How many heirs fit the profile, and what does it cost to sign one? (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo)
- What does a field station cost per plot, and does the base fee cover it? (/founder-cfo)
- What does the company own that a grower partner cannot take away?
- Which agent beats a good human manager, and how would you prove it?
- Why is this still the trusted name in ten years?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Name the moat in one sentence and build it first. The likeliest candidate is an independent audit brand (sealed weighing plus yield record) that the company owns, with contracts that keep the heir relationship with the company.
2. Sign the pilot plus at least 5 non-family heirs, recording the real cost of winning each.
3. Stay in one district with durian and rubber until it holds most of the heirs it can reach, and delay the platform until then.
4. Get the competitor map and per-plot unit economics before spending on infrastructure beyond the pilot.
