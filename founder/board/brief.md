# Board brief: Surat Smart Land (round 4, "Farm Continuity Cover")

## The customer and the must-have moment

Farm families in Surat Thani, southern Thailand, growing durian and rubber (ยางพารา):
- **The parents** still farm and live on the land.
- **The children** (2nd/3rd generation, often in Bangkok) will inherit. They don't know how to farm, don't want to learn, want to keep the land and its income, and value comfort and trust.

A real example: the founder's partner's family farms durian and rubber, and the partner has said that if she inherits she wouldn't know how to run it.

**The must-have moment is the sudden handover, not the slow one:** a parent gets sick, injured or dies, often just before durian harvest, and the children in Bangkok have no idea who the crews, buyers or suppliers are, or what needs doing this week. One lost durian harvest can be very costly (the size is unverified).

## What earlier rounds learned (the board scored earlier versions 4–4.7/10)

- Smart-farming tools for parents who already farm well were not a must-have.
- Security is redundant for farmers who live on the land.
- The payer was unclear, and the payoff was years away.
- Grower partners could bypass the company.
- There was too much technology for the budget.
- The founder has since parked the AI agent platform.

## The model

### 1. Farm Continuity Cover (core product, paid by the heir)
- **Farm will:** the parents' month-by-month know-how, crews, buyers, input suppliers and boundary pins, captured through voice interviews, phone photos and a LINE log, and kept up to date.
- **Activation:** any named person (heir, parent, or a trusted village contact such as the village headman) sends "parent can't work" on LINE. A vetted local crew leader is on site within 48 hours and runs the recorded routine for up to 90 days, with daily photos in the family LINE group, while the family decides what to do.
- **Crew standby:** 2–3 local crew leaders per cluster on small monthly retainers. These are existing district contract crews and retired or part-time growers, not employees.
- **In normal times:** a weekly plain-Thai LINE report to the heirs.

### 2. Village hub (delivery and cost base)

One hub for a cluster of 30–40 neighbouring farms:
- pooled fertiliser and fungicide orders delivered on the crop-calendar date
- a peak-season dispatch board for durian cutting crews and tappers
- a monthly agronomist round of all farms
- a certified scale where harvests are weighed and sealed

Grower partners and tappers carry an honesty score (reported weight against the sealed scale). The heir-signed agreement says shares are calculated on hub weights.

### 3. Later: buyer-paid verified lots

Durian buyers (ล้ง / packing houses exporting to China) pay a per-kg fee for lots with tree-tagged maturity dates, a photo chain of custody and GAP paperwork done, which reduces rejections for immature fruit. Rubber EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) mapping comes later still.

### Role and guarantee

- The company advises and coordinates.
- **No guarantee on crops, weather or prices.**
- **Service guarantee:** a crew leader on site within 48 hours, or the fees are refunded.

## Price (estimates, to be tested)

- **Continuity Cover:** about ฿990–2,500 per farm per month, paid by the heir.
- **During activation:** crew wages paid from the farm's own revenue, plus a 10–15% management fee.
- **Farm will as a one-off product:** ฿15,000–30,000.
- **Hub:**
  - an 8–12% margin on pooled inputs
  - ฿300–500 per crew-day booked in peak weeks
  - 1–2% on harvest weighed and sold through the hub
  - an optional ฿500–1,000/month heir subscription for sealed records
- **Later:** buyers pay about ฿1–2/kg for verified lots.

## Plan and budget

- Bootstrapped, under ฿1M, founder(s) only. The year-1 hub estimate is ฿500–700k: certified scale, rented shed space, LINE plus spreadsheets, a part-time agronomist, one runner.
- **First 30 days:**
  - Build the first farm will on the pilot family farm.
  - Show it to 15–20 Bangkok heirs and ask for a refundable ฿3,000 founding deposit. Success = 5 deposits.
  - Get 2 crew leaders to sign standby terms.
  - Run one pooled fertiliser order with 10+ farms.
  - Ask 2 packing houses for a price on a GAP-documented cluster.

## Not known yet

- How many such families there are and how much the heirs would pay monthly.
- How often parents become unable to work.
- Crew availability in peak durian season.
- Whether packing houses will pay at least the middleman price.
- Real set-up costs, and competitors (local middlemen who advance cash to parents, existing farm managers, insurers).
